
# Elvis Kibet Ngetich

**Software Engineering · Artificial Intelligence · Computer Science · Human Potential**

*Building from where I am, learning through what I build, and working toward what I believe is possible.*

I am Elvis Kibet Ngetich, a Kenyan aspiring software engineer and AI builder interested in the intersection of technology, medicine, and human potential.

My journey into software engineering began in 2022. It has included academic setbacks, financial constraints, mentorship, community, self-directed learning, and a determination to keep building despite an unconventional path.

I believe meaningful engineering begins with understanding real problems, learning the fundamentals, testing ideas, and building solutions that people can actually use.

## The Beginning: A Dream Formed in 2022

My ambition to pursue Computer Science grew through the influence of people whose journeys helped me see possibilities beyond my immediate circumstances. My cousin and mentor, Kevin Yatich Rono, particularly influenced my understanding of Computer Science as a field that can intersect with many other disciplines.

I have always been drawn to both Computer Science and Medicine. I recognise that pursuing both as full degrees simultaneously is not a simple path, but their intersection continues to interest me: intelligent systems, health technology, medical innovation, and technology that can improve people's lives.

Yale University remains one of my long-term academic aspirations. The destination matters to me, but so does the work required to become ready for the opportunities I hope to pursue.

## Kabianga, KCSE, and an Unexpected Detour

I completed my secondary education at Kabianga High School in 2023. I had hoped to achieve an A plain and had communicated that ambition to my cousin. My KCSE result was a C plain, far below what I had expected for myself.

The result was difficult to accept. I later repeated my examinations at Cheptenye Boys High School in 2025 and improved to a C+, but I still had not reached the academic outcome I wanted.

My school experience was shaped by more than examinations. My parents were supporting four children in different schools, and meeting school fees was a significant challenge for our family. I valued the structured, conducive learning environment at Kabianga, where I felt comfortable studying. At home, however, access to books, resources, and a suitable study environment was limited.

I also experienced anxiety during examinations and eye problems, including excessive tearing during my KCSE period. I believe these difficulties affected my experience, although I cannot establish that they caused my examination results.

The gap between my ambitions and my results affected my confidence, particularly when I watched friends progress to university while I remained at home trying to determine my next step.

I am still learning to accept that an academic setback can change a route without necessarily ending a person's ambitions.

## 2024: When Technology Became Tangible

In 2024, a schoolmate's early prototype, FarmConnectInn, made my interest in software development feel more tangible.

The idea was to connect farmers with opportunities and help them unlock their earning potential. What interested me was not simply the website itself, but the possibility of turning an idea into something useful.

At the time, I had a strong desire to participate in building technology, but limited equipment, experience, and resources. I began to appreciate the distance between having an idea, understanding how to implement it, and developing a reliable product.

FarmConnectInn became one of the experiences that helped me see what a group of people with complementary skills might accomplish. I hope that, as our skills and resources grow, ideas like these can be revisited and developed into valuable solutions.

It strengthened a belief I still hold: innovation does not have to begin with a large budget or an established company. It can begin with a real problem, a simple prototype, and people willing to learn.

## The Ivy League Ambition

I explored preparing for the SAT and applying to Ivy League universities, including Yale. The cost of examinations, English-language testing where required, application fees, preparation, and the wider application process became a major barrier.

My KCSE results also meant that certain scholarship pathways I had hoped to pursue were not available to me in the way I had imagined.

Eventually, I put the immediate application plan aside. That decision reflected my financial circumstances, not the disappearance of the ambition.

I still hope to pursue Computer Science, explore its relationship with Medicine, and apply directly to suitable universities when I can prepare a competitive application and manage the costs. I also hope to identify legitimate financial-aid opportunities and application-fee waivers where available.

Whether that opportunity comes before or after I turn 30, I intend to keep learning and preparing. Yale remains a destination I hope to reach, not an outcome I take for granted.

## A Problem I Cannot Ignore: Poverty, Betting, and the Search for Hope

In the communities around me, I have seen how deeply sports betting and
jackpot gambling can become tied to the search for financial security.
For some young people, a bet is not merely entertainment. It becomes
a desperate attempt to pay school fees, escape poverty, support family,
or change circumstances that seem impossible to overcome through
ordinary means.

I understand why the promise of a life-changing win can be attractive
when legitimate opportunities feel distant. But gambling can also deepen
the very financial hardship people are trying to escape, particularly
when losses lead to repeated betting, borrowed money, or the pursuit of
one more win.

I do not want to approach this problem by blaming or humiliating people.
I want to understand the economic pressure, lack of opportunity, and
desire for hope behind it.

This is one of the reasons I am interested in the intersection of
technology, human behaviour, and human potential. I want to explore
whether responsible digital tools, practical financial education,
accessible learning opportunities, and pathways to earning through
real skills can help young people make more informed decisions and
discover alternatives to risking money they cannot afford to lose.

Technology alone cannot solve poverty or gambling addiction. But it
can potentially contribute to a wider response—one that treats people
with dignity, addresses underlying pressures, and makes constructive
alternatives more accessible.

## A Broader Vision for African Technology

I want to contribute to technology empowerment in Kenya and across Africa, whether through communities such as PLP or through initiatives I may eventually help organise independently.

My longer-term vision is to bring learners together around real problems, help them strengthen their technical foundations, and guide collaborative projects from problem discovery through design, implementation, testing, and improvement.

I am especially interested in people who have ambitions in technology but lack the resources or conventional educational opportunities to pursue them. Some never enter university; others cannot afford college or specialist training. Many also dream of studying abroad and returning with knowledge that can help address challenges in their communities.

I believe that collaboration can make these journeys less isolating.

The practical starting point is simple: identify a real problem, understand the people affected, build a small solution, test it, learn from the results, and improve it. Over time, a disciplined group can turn small experiments into useful products and sustainable initiatives.

This is a direction I hope to develop through experience. It is not a claim that I have already built an established organisation or achieved these ambitions.

My long-term ambition is to build technology around problems people
actually experience: young people who cannot afford further education,
learners without access to computers or structured training, families
under financial pressure, and individuals who turn to gambling because
they see few other routes forward.

I want to contribute to an environment where young people can discover
their abilities, develop marketable skills, access credible opportunities,
and make better-informed decisions about their futures. My goal is not
to promise a shortcut out of poverty, but to help create more realistic
paths towards opportunity, independence, and dignity.
